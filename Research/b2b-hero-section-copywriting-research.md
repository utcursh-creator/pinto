## SECTION 1: Expert Copywriter Frameworks for Hero Sections

### 1A. Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers) — The Queen of Conversion Copy

**Core Philosophy: "Instead of writing your message, steal it. Steal it directly from your prospects."**

Wiebe's entire system is built on the idea that if you're sitting at your desk trying to figure out what to say, you're doing it wrong. Your customers can write your copy for you — at least your first draft. The first five parts of her conversion copywriting process are 100% about the prospect. You barely write copy at all. Most of what you'll write is swiped from "voice of customer" data and paired with swiped formulas.

#### The Specificity Rule
Wiebe's #1 headline principle. Vague promises lose sales; details earn trust.

- BAD: "We help you grow your business"
- GOOD: "We helped a London-based creative agency generate 143 qualified leads in 12 months"

Specificity tells the prospect: "This is real. This has happened. This could work for you, too." Name the pain, the outcome, the transformation. Drop in a product name instead of saying "our solution."

The actionable tip: include concrete details in your headlines — a number, a timeframe, a named outcome.

#### Headlines Do 80% of the Work
If the headline fails, nothing else gets read. Wiebe teaches maintaining swipe files and developing repeatable headline formulas.

#### Headline Formulas (from Copy School)
- "The Only Way to [Do Something Desirable] Without [Doing Something Undesirable]"
  - Example: "The Only Way to Turn Off the Lights Without Clapping or Getting Out of Bed"
- "[Do Something Hard] in [Period of Time] or [Promise]"
  - Example: "Tune Your Piano in 15 Minutes or 'Piano Tuner App' Is Free"
- "[Do Something Desirable] Like [an Expert] Without [Something Expected & Undesirable]"
  - Example: "Learn to Play Chess Like Bobby Fischer — Without Any of the Crazy!"

#### The PAS Framework on Home Pages (Case Study: 49% Lift)
Wiebe tested PAS (Problem-Agitation-Solution) against the standard value-prop-first approach on SweatBlock's home page:
- **Problem**: Present the core issue your audience faces
- **Agitation**: Intensify the emotional pain around that problem
- **Solution**: Introduce your product as the relief

Result: **PAS variations produced a 46-49% paid conversion lift** with 99% confidence over the established control (which had already doubled revenue).

Key insight: Most home pages try to serve all stages of awareness simultaneously, diluting messaging. Wiebe's approach: identify your most convertible audience segment and write specifically for their awareness level.

**Critical distinction**: Wiebe notes that using PAS on a home page means you can't do what most home pages do — lead with the solution. Almost every home page leads with the product or solution. PAS flips this by leading with the problem. This is counterintuitive but proven.

#### The Review Mining Process (Voice of Customer Gold)
1. Ask four foundational questions: Who is my target audience? What solutions do they want? What solutions do they use today? Where are those solutions found online?
2. Identify review sources (Amazon books, Reddit, competitor reviews, support communities)
3. Mine systematically — Wiebe reviewed 500+ reviews, extracting direct quotes with "as few summaries or abstractions as possible"
4. Identify patterns — recurring language, frustrations, and motivations rise to the top
5. Test headlines using extracted language

Case study: Her winning rehab center headline — "If you think you need rehab, you do" — came directly from review language and delivered **400% more clicks** than the control.

#### 10 Conversion Copywriting Principles (from Copy School)
1. **VoC Research Is an Unfair Advantage** — Conduct 5-10 customer interviews (60 min each), create surveys, review support tickets. "If research isn't your biggest task, you're probably doing it wrong."
2. **Clarity Beats Cleverness (Usually)** — Aim for the intersection of understandable and memorable.
3. **Frameworks Cure Blank-Page Anxiety** — PAS, DOS, AIDA, Transformation Message Map.
4. **Every Line Has One Clear Job** — Read copy aloud: "Does this give the reader a reason to keep going?"
5. **Empathy Is Your Conversion Superpower** — "Your job isn't to push, but to gently guide." Trust drives conversion.
6. **Headlines Do 80% of the Work** — Use the Specificity Rule.
7. **Buttons Are Conversion Gateways** — Write from the prospect's perspective. Not "Learn More" but "Send me the free guide."
8. **Risk Reduction Builds Confidence** — Zero Risk Sweep: identify where doubt exists, address with guarantees, proof, and transparency.
9. **Specificity Builds Trust** — Replace vague language with concrete details, numbers, named outcomes.
10. **Write Like a Good Therapist** — Ask what's preventing the prospect from saying yes. Validate objections, name them, gently reframe.

#### Value Proposition Formulas (for Hero Headlines)
- **Value-specific**: "My product is the one that ___________" — fill with specific value delivered
  - Example: "Reclaim creates the perfect schedule for your priorities, saving you up to 40% of your workweek"
- **Who-specific**: "The only/best/easy X for Y" — X is your offer, Y is your target audience

### 1B. Eddie Shleyner (VeryGoodCopy) — The Curiosity Architect

**Core Philosophy: "Knowing how to create curiosity is one of the most important copywriting and marketing skills you can develop."**

#### The Curiosity Gap Headline Formula (3 Steps)
1. **Make a valuable promise** — Establish what benefit readers will gain
2. **Tease the value** — Create intrigue by hinting at the mechanism without fully revealing it
3. **Embellish the benefit** — Amplify the desirability

Example (Joe Sugarman's BluBlocker ad — $20M in sales):
- Promise: "Vision Breakthrough"
- Tease: "When I put on the pair of glasses..."
- Embellish: "...what I saw I could not believe. Nor will you."

#### Fascinations
Sentences designed to conjure intense curiosity and compel action. Invented by copywriting legend Mel Martin in the 1960s, used by Gary Halbert and Ben Settle.

#### Creative Process: "The Well"
Shleyner maintains a repository of ideas, each with 4 components:
1. A working headline or title
2. A narrative or anecdote
3. A copywriting lesson or tip
4. A word count

His definition of creativity = **connectivity** — putting things together in new combinations.

### 1C. Harry Dry (Marketing Examples) — The Simplicity Enforcer

**Core Philosophy: "A great sentence is a good sentence made shorter. Any word that isn't working for you is working against you."**

#### The Three Rules of Great Copy

**Rule 1: Make it Concrete (Visualizable)**
People see in pictures. Concrete, vivid images stick far better than abstract concepts.
- BAD: "Productive routines"
- GOOD: "Waking up at 6am to write"

**Rule 2: Make it Falsifiable**
When you write a sentence that's true or false, you put your head on the chopping block. It makes people sit up in their seat. Falsifiable statements carry authenticity and credibility because they can be fact-checked.

**Rule 3: Make it Unique**
If any competitor can say the same thing, you haven't truly differentiated yourself.

**The Gold Standard Example**: New Balance's tagline — "Worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio"
- Highly visualizable (you can see both images)
- Falsifiable (specific, verifiable claims)
- Unique (no competitor could say this)

#### Landing Page Title Principles

**The 2-Second Test**: "1 Mississippi, 2 Mississippi. You get it?" If your main point isn't clear within two seconds, it's too complex or abstract.

**The 24-Hour Test**: Write 10 title variations, show to friends, ignore their feedback, wait a full day, then ask which one they remember. Memorability trumps likability.

**Conviction over Hedging**:
- Don't say "We help" — say "It's how"
- Don't say "alternative" — say "replaces"
- Don't prioritize poetic or clever phrasing over clarity

**Match Your Audience's Mindset**:
- Cold leads (unaware of problems): Lead with the problem
- Warm leads (actively seeking solutions): Lead with the solution

**A paragraph is ready to ship when there's nothing left to remove.**

### 1D. Joel Klettke (Business Casual Copywriting) — The VoC Practitioner

**Core Philosophy: "Real conversion copywriters don't guess."**

#### His Own Hero Section (Masterclass in Itself)
Headline: "I'm a SaaS and B2B copywriter who thinks words are boring."
Follow-up: "But copy — really great copy? The kind that sells things? That's sexy stuff."

Then specific social proof: "HubSpot (2x conversions), Deputy (2x sign-ups to best-converting funnel), and WP Engine."

Structure: Attention-grabber → Credibility (named clients with metrics) → Methodology (research-driven, results-focused)

#### The Before-During-After Framework
Used for both case studies and sales copy:
- **Before**: Set the stakes. Introduce a human hero. What was the pain? What was broken?
- **During**: What was done and why it was valuable. Showcase thinking and problem-solving approach.
- **After**: KPIs/metrics and the impact of the change.

Key insight: Business decisions are still human decisions.

#### VoC Mining Techniques
Three mechanisms:
1. **Interviewing** — Phone or face-to-face conversations
2. **Surveys** — Through sites or emails
3. **Wild mining** — Testimonials, reviews, Reddit conversations

What to look for: Questions people ask, how they defend or tear down services, what anxieties they raise and how they're countered. This lets you counter objections directly on the page.

**The Mirror Effect**: When you use the exact language your customers use, it creates a mirror where prospects see themselves. It's a massive empathy-building exercise.

## SECTION 3: Psychology of the Buyer at the Moment They Land

### 3A. The Business Owner Searching for AI Implementation Help (Audience A)

**Mental State:**
- **Frustrated**: They've tried AI tools. Maybe ChatGPT, maybe something their IT person set up. It's not sticking. They've spent money and time with little to show.
- **Skeptical**: They've heard the hype. They've seen the demos. But their reality looks nothing like the demos.
- **Afraid of looking foolish**: They know AI is important but feel behind. Their competitors might be ahead. But they don't want to throw more money at something that won't work.
- **Overwhelmed by options**: Every vendor promises the moon. They can't tell real capability from marketing hype.
- **Time-pressured**: They're running a business. They don't have time to become AI experts.

**The Adoption Problem (from HBR research):**
- Fear of replacement: Employees resist because they see AI as a threat, not a tool
- Passive non-compliance: Low usage rates, skipped training, disengagement
- Lack of clear communication: Creates distrust — employees see AI as a threat instead of an innovation
- Misalignment with daily work: New tools that aren't connected to broader organizational goals seem like random changes
- The solution that works: Involving people from the start. When they understand AI is a tool to boost productivity, not a threat to their job, it changes the tone completely.

**What they need to see in the first 5 seconds:**
- "This company understands MY problem" (not generic AI hype)
- Evidence they've done this before (specificity, not abstraction)
- A path that doesn't require them to become technical
- Quick time-to-value (not a 6-month engagement before results)

### 3B. The Agency Founder Who Needs Fulfillment Capacity (Audience B)

**Mental State:**
- **Anxious about delivery**: They've sold AI services they can't fulfill. The gap between what they promised and what they can deliver is growing.
- **Revenue under threat**: Every project they can't deliver is revenue they lose — or worse, reputation damage.
- **Hiring fatigue**: They've tried hiring developers. The talent market is expensive, slow, and unreliable. Internal hires take months to ramp.
- **Control concerns**: They want to maintain their brand, their client relationships, their pricing. They don't want a partner who will steal their clients.
- **Quality paranoia**: Their name is on the deliverable. A bad fulfillment partner destroys their reputation.

**Key Decision Factors (from research):**
- Cost of in-house: A full in-house AI specialist costs 3-4x the annual cost of a white-label partnership (salary, benefits, training, management, tools)
- Retention: Agencies using white-label partners retain clients 42% longer than in-house delivery models
- Revenue stability: 22% less revenue volatility during client downturns
- Primary concern: Maintaining high-quality deliverables under their own brand

**What they need to see in the first 5 seconds:**
- "You can sell it. We build and deploy it. Your brand stays."
- Evidence of quality (case studies, named outcomes)
- Clear operational model (not vague partnership language)
- Speed of delivery (they have clients waiting NOW)

### 3C. Eugene Schwartz's Awareness Levels — Where Your Visitors Are

**The Five Stages:**

| Stage | Description | % of Market | Hero Copy Approach |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Unaware | Don't know they have a problem | Large | Content marketing, not landing page |
| Problem-Aware | Know the problem, not the solution | ~20% | Lead with the problem. Empathetic language. Create a bit of mystery. |
| Solution-Aware | Know solutions exist, don't know YOUR solution | ~10% | Lead with differentiation. Why your approach is best. |
| Product-Aware | Know your product, on the fence | Small | Lead with your product. Social proof. Overcome objections. |
| Most Aware | Ready to buy, just need a push | Small | Lead with the offer. CTA-forward. |

**For this website, most visitors will be Problem-Aware or Solution-Aware:**
- Problem-Aware (Audience A): "We bought AI tools but our team won't use them. We need someone to make this actually work."
- Solution-Aware (Audience B): "We know we need a fulfillment partner. We're comparing options."

**Copy implication:** The hero should meet Problem-Aware visitors (lead with their pain in their language) while being specific enough to signal to Solution-Aware visitors that this is their answer.

### 3D. The Emotional Triggers That Make B2B Buyers Scroll

**The Big Insight**: B2B buyers are MORE emotionally invested than B2C buyers (Google research). B2B purchasers are **50% more likely** to buy when they see personal value — career advancement, recognition, feeling competent. They are **8x more likely to pay a premium** when personal value is present.

**Emotions that drive B2B decisions:**
- **Fear of failure** dominates B2B buying (not FOMO). The fear of messing up (FOMU) is stronger than the fear of missing out.
- **Career risk**: They're accountable to superiors, peers, and subordinates. A bad vendor choice can damage their career.
- **Status quo bias**: "Inaction is guiltless." The failure to decide is collective, not individual. Sticking with the status quo is perceived as the least-risk option.
- **Gut feeling**: Even when buyers think they're making rational choices, their initial emotional response colors how they interpret data.

**The Two-Part Solution (from JOLT Effect research):**
1. **Dial up FOMO** to overcome status quo bias — make doing nothing feel expensive
2. **Dial down FOMU** to overcome indecision — make the buying process feel safe

For hero copy this means: Quantify the cost of inaction (what they're losing by NOT solving this) AND reduce perceived risk (show them exactly what happens when they say yes).

## SECTION 4: Specific Copywriting Techniques for Hero Sections

### 4A. Conversion Research on Above-the-Fold Elements

**The Nielsen Norman Stat**: Users spend **57% of their viewing time** above the fold. This is prime real estate.

**The 3-Second Rule**: Headlines should take no more than 3 seconds to understand what your tool does and why it matters.

**Recommended Above-the-Fold Hierarchy:**

1. **Headline** — One powerful sentence conveying the core benefit. Not what you do, but what they get. "Cut Project Timelines by 50%" beats "Project Management Software."
2. **Subheadline** — One layer of detail or proof. How you deliver on the headline promise. Example: "200+ websites built on time and on budget since 2006."
3. **Primary CTA** — Within first 1,200 pixels. Value-driven language: "Schedule Your Free Demo," "Get Your Custom Quote." Not "Learn More" or "Contact Us."
4. **Social Proof** — Customer logos, trust bar, or a single powerful testimonial. Placed just below or within the hero.

**CTA Research:**
- Too many CTAs can decrease conversion by up to **266%**
- Moving primary CTA from mid-page to above the fold = **28% lift** in demo bookings (fintech case study)
- Personalized CTAs convert **202% better**

**Social Proof Data:**
- Adding 3 customer testimonials = **34% increase** in conversion rates
- Logos alone = **43% increase**; logos + testimonials = **84% increase**
- Video testimonials = **80% conversion improvement** over text
- **79% of B2B buyers** rely on social proof when making decisions
- Most effective: Specific quotes mentioning outcomes, with customer names, job titles, and company logos

**Common Mistake**: Cramming multiple messages, CTAs, excessive copy, and competing visuals into a single space.

### 4B. Headline Length and Structure

**The Data:**
- B2B hero headlines that convert tend to be 4-9 words (based on Palantir, Scale AI, Anduril analysis)
- Headlines must answer: "What is this, and why should I care?"
- Conviction language outperforms hedging: "It's how" > "We help"; "replaces" > "alternative"
- Specific beats abstract: "Project Management Software That Doubles Team Productivity" > "Revolutionize the Way You Work"

### 4C. Emotional vs. Rational Appeals — The Data

**Long-term**: Emotional appeals drive **7x more** large business effects than rational campaigns (sales, profits, revenue)

**Short-term**: Rational messaging produces roughly **2x more** short-term activation effects (sales uplifts, MQLs)

**The Optimal Strategy**: Start with emotional branding for awareness, transition to rational messaging for evaluation and purchase. For a hero section, this means:
- **Headline**: Emotional (problem-aware, feeling-driven)
- **Subheadline**: Rational (specifics, how it works, proof)
- **CTA**: Action-oriented with risk reduction

### 4D. What NOT to Do (Anti-Patterns)

- Don't use "Revolutionize" or "Transform" in the headline — it's the most overused word in B2B
- Don't describe your product; describe the outcome
- Don't use jargon ("leverage," "synergy," "end-to-end")
- Don't have more than one primary CTA above the fold
- Don't skip social proof — 79% of B2B buyers rely on it
- Don't write for everyone — write for your most convertible audience segment (Wiebe principle)
- Don't be clever at the cost of clarity

## SECTION 5: Voice of Customer Research Approach

### 5A. How an Expert Copywriter Would Approach This Hero

**Step 1: Research First, Write Last**
Per Wiebe: "If research isn't your biggest task, you're probably doing it wrong."

An expert would NOT start by writing headlines. They would start by:
1. Interviewing 5-10 existing clients (60 minutes each)
2. Mining online reviews, Reddit threads, competitor reviews
3. Reading support tickets and sales call transcripts
4. Surveying prospects on thank-you pages
5. Reviewing competitor positioning to find gaps

**Step 2: Identify Dominant Themes**
Across all VoC sources, look for:
- Recurring frustrations (what's broken)
- Trigger events (what made them start looking)
- Language patterns (how they describe their problem)
- Desired outcomes (what "success" looks like to them)
- Objections and anxieties (what held them back)
- Alternatives tried (what they did before)

**Step 3: Create Messaging Hierarchy**
What matters MOST goes in the headline. What matters next goes in the subheadline. The rest flows down the page in priority order.

### 5B. The VoC Interview Questions That Unlock Headlines

**Before Questions (The Gold Mine):**
- "What was going on in your life/business that brought you to [solution] today?"
- "What problems did you face before our solution, and how did this affect your business?"
- "When did you realize you had this problem?"
- "What did you do, or what solutions did you try, before using us?"
- "What made you seek out or consider us?"
- "Why did you choose us over other options?"

**During Questions:**
- "What was your experience like?"
- "What benefits, features, or results did you particularly value?"

**After Questions:**
- "What results have you seen?"
- "What would you tell someone who's on the fence?"

**The "Magic Question" (from Copyhackers):**
"What was going on in your life that brought you to X today?" — This surfaces the trigger event and emotional state that drives the buying decision.

### 5C. How to Translate VoC Into Headlines

**The Process:**
1. Extract verbatim language from interviews/reviews — keep their casual, authentic voice
2. Organize into a spreadsheet with columns: Pains, Desires, Trigger Events, Language Patterns
3. Identify the 3-5 most repeated themes
4. Test "real-life" copy in prominent places: headlines, subheadlines, opening body text
5. Keep formal or technical copy farther down the page

**The Mirror Principle:**
When you use the exact language your customers use, those words resonate with other similar prospects. It creates a mirror where prospects see themselves. Mirroring is one of the most effective ways to build rapport — when you talk like your customers talk, they like you more, and when you use their exact language, they feel completely understood.

### 5D. What to Listen for — Specific to Audience A and Audience B

**For Audience A (Business Owners with AI Adoption Problems):**
Listen for phrases like:
- "We tried X but it didn't stick"
- "My team won't use it"
- "We spent money on AI tools and nobody touches them"
- "I don't have time to figure out the technical stuff"
- "I need someone who gets my business, not just the technology"
- "We're falling behind our competitors"
- "I know AI can help but I don't know where to start"
- "The demos looked great but reality is different"

**For Audience B (Agency Founders Needing Fulfillment):**
Listen for phrases like:
- "I can sell it but I can't build it"
- "I'm turning down projects because I don't have the capacity"
- "My developer quit and I have three clients waiting"
- "I need someone who can deliver under my brand"
- "Quality has to be there — my reputation is on the line"
- "I don't want to hire full-time for something that fluctuates"
- "I need a partner who won't steal my clients"
- "Speed matters — my clients expected this yesterday"

## SECTION 6: Synthesis — How All of This Applies to the Vibelife Hero

### 6A. The Strategic Decision: Who Are You Writing For?

Wiebe's principle: Don't write for everyone. Write for your most convertible audience segment.

**Option 1**: Lead with Audience A (business owners) in the hero, address Audience B in a secondary section
**Option 2**: Lead with Audience B (agency fulfillment) in the hero, address Audience A in a secondary section
**Option 3**: Find the common thread and write a hero that speaks to both

The common thread: **Both audiences have a gap between what they want AI to do and what it actually does.** Both need someone who will embed with them and make it work — not sell them software and disappear.

### 6B. Framework Synthesis for the Hero

Applying all the expert frameworks to this specific case:

**Wiebe's PAS + Specificity Rule:**
- Problem: You have AI tools but they're not producing results
- Agitation: Every week you don't fix this, you're losing [specific thing]
- Solution: We embed engineers in your operation and make AI actually work

**Dry's Three Rules Test:**
- Is it concrete/visual? Can you picture it happening?
- Is it falsifiable? Could you prove it true or false?
- Is it unique? Could any competitor say this exact thing?

**Shleyner's Curiosity Formula:**
- Promise: What will the visitor get?
- Tease: What's the mechanism they haven't heard of?
- Embellish: What's the surprising outcome?

**Klettke's Before-After Test:**
- Before: What is the reader's state right now? (frustrated, skeptical, time-poor)
- After: What does life look like after working with you? (AI producing results, team using it, revenue growing)

**Schwartz's Awareness Match:**
- Most visitors are Problem-Aware → lead with the problem
- Subheadline transitions to solution
- Social proof provides product-aware validation

### 6C. The Palantir-Inspired Positioning Angle

What makes Palantir's model relevant:
- **Not advisory** — they don't recommend. They build.
- **Not software-only** — they don't ship a product and disappear. They embed.
- **Forward deployed** — engineers sit inside the client's operation.
- **Outcome-accountable** — they stay until it works in production.

This maps directly to the vibelife positioning:
- Don't say "consulting" — say "we embed with your team"
- Don't say "we implement" — say "we build inside your business"
- Don't say "we advise" — say "we deploy and we stay until it works"

OpenAI's positioning provides validation: "No slide decks. Just working AI inside your systems."

### 6D. Risk Reduction in the Hero (Critical for Both Audiences)

Both audiences are afraid of making another bad bet. The hero must reduce perceived risk:

**Techniques from the research:**
1. **Wiebe's Zero Risk Sweep**: Identify every point of doubt and address it
2. **JOLT Effect**: Show them exactly what happens step by step (detailed project plan reduces FOMU)
3. **Social proof placement**: Logos + specific outcome testimonials above the fold
4. **Speed signal**: "From zero to use case in days" (Palantir's own language)
5. **Specificity as trust**: Name the outcome, name the timeframe, name the method
